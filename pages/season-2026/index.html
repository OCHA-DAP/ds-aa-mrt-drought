Summary

Return period = (number of seasons + 1) ÷ rank, 2001–2026 (26 seasons). Rank 1 gives 1-in-27, the longest this record can show. Vegetation is compared with its 2001–2025 trend because it has greened over the period; the plain comparison is given alongside.

Method and sources

Sources and dates
    How each indicator is computed
    • Common record. Every indicator is ranked on 2001–2026, the framework's analysis period and the first year all five series exist. Each year uses the same dates as 2026. Return period = (n + 1) ÷ rank (Weibull), with n = 26. On CHIRPS's full record (1981–2026) July–September 2026 ranks lower, because the 1980s and early 1990s were drier: .
    • Areas. National is the framework's trigger area: the average over every pixel in Mauritania, so about half of it is Saharan. The southern belt is the eight wilayas FAO monitors for crops (Hodh Ech Chargui, Hodh El Gharbi, Assaba, Guidimakha, Gorgol, Brakna, Tagant, Trarza), averaged by area (FAO's vegetation health: unweighted, as FAO publishes no cropland area per wilaya). Guidimakha is where the framework's activities are.
    • Rainfall. CHIRPS dekadal totals by wilaya (WFP, on HDX), summed July–September (as in the framework analysis) and June–October. September is the last "final" month; October is preliminary. The Window 2 check uses the framework's five national thresholds on cumulative rain since 1 July. Re-run on 2001–2025 with these data it is met in 2002, 2011, 2014, 2017 and 2019 only, which is the framework's own backtest (5 of 9 bad years, no false alarms). The framework had already activated on its forecast window in May 2026, so this check is a reading of today's data, not an official result.
    • Biomass. GeoSahel dry matter production (Copernicus DMP, processed by Action contre la Faim), summed from 1 April (dekad 10) to 30 September (dekad 27), the last dekad published. Compared with a linear trend fitted on 2001–2025, as in the Chad framework's biomass trigger. GeoSahel's own 2026 total on its portal completes the year with average values and differs from this.
    • Crops. FAO ASIS publishes the annual Agricultural Stress Index (the share of cropland where vegetation health over the season stays below 35%) after the season ends; 2026 is not out yet. The 2026 ranking uses ASIS's dekadal weighted mean Vegetation Health Index on cropland, averaged over each wilaya's season dekads between 21 June and 1 November (the same dekads every year), compared with its 2001–2025 trend. Its season mean ranks years much as the official ASI does (Spearman ρ against national ASI, 2001–2025).
    • Rangeland. JRC ASAP's z-score of cumulative FPAR on rangeland during the growing cycle, at , the latest dekad with data for every wilaya in every year. ASAP scores it against its whole record, which carries the greening trend, so it is compared with its 2001–2025 trend. This is satellite vegetation like the biomass indicator, not independent evidence.
    • Surface water. GeoSahel monthly open-water area (km²) by wilaya and moughataa, October values, October 2020 onwards. Moughataas with under 0.5 km² of water on average are left blank.
    • Temperature. JRC ASAP's ECMWF reanalysis temperature on the rangeland mask, July–September mean, compared with a Theil–Sen trend fitted on 1989–2025. In Chad and Nigeria this year ERA5 rain ran far drier than CHIRPS, which can make a reanalysis run hot. In Mauritania ECMWF rain (FAO ASIS, July–September 2026, % of 2001–2025 average, ECMWF vs CHIRPS) is drier than CHIRPS only in Hodh Ech Chargui: . The hottest wilayas, Brakna and Trarza, are ones where ECMWF rain is as wet as CHIRPS or wetter, so the heat is not an artefact of a dry reanalysis.
    • Wilaya maps. ASAP and FAO report on older GAUL units, matched to COD-AB v03 wilayas by name. ASAP has no separate Inchiri, Tiris Zemmour or Nouakchott; FAO covers only the southern belt. CHIRPS and ECMWF rainfall agree only loosely at wilaya scale (r for July–September, 2001–2025), so read single-wilaya rainfall ranks with care.
