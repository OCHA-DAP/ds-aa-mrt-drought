Summary

Return period = (number of seasons + 1) ÷ rank, 2001–2026; rank 1 gives 1-in-27, the longest this record can show. Vegetation is shown against the 2001–2025 average and, smaller, against its linear trend: the years since 2020 were much greener, so the two readings differ. Crop stress under 1% of cropland counts as none when ranking.

Method and sources

Sources and dates
    How each indicator is computed
    • Common record. Every indicator is ranked on 2001–2026, the framework's analysis period and the first year all the series exist; each year uses the same dates as 2026. Return period = (n + 1) ÷ rank (Weibull), with n = 26.
    • Areas. National is the framework's trigger area: the average over every pixel in Mauritania, about half of it Saharan. The southern belt is the eight wilayas FAO monitors for crops (Hodh Ech Chargui, Hodh El Gharbi, Assaba, Guidimakha, Gorgol, Brakna, Tagant, Trarza), averaged by area. FAO's crop indicators are weighted by cropland: ASIS's share of each wilaya used in the computation times the wilaya's area ( of the belt's cropland). Guidimakha is where the framework's activities are.
    • Rainfall. CHIRPS dekadal totals by wilaya (WFP, on HDX), summed July–September (as in the framework analysis) and June–October. September is the last "final" month; October is preliminary. The Window 2 check uses the framework's five national thresholds on cumulative rain since 1 July. Re-run on 2001–2025 with these data it is met in 2002, 2011, 2014, 2017 and 2019 only, as in the framework's own backtest (5 of 9 bad years, no false alarms).
    • Vegetation and the trend. Biomass, FAO VHI and ASAP's rangeland z-score all rose over 2001–2025, but mostly as a step up since 2020, not a steady trend (biomass: not significant). Each is shown against the plain 2001–2025 average and against a linear trend fitted on 2001–2025 (the Chad framework's biomass trigger uses a ratio to trend). The plain reading is the default. Neither separates the framework's bad years clearly better than the other.
    • Biomass. GeoSahel dry matter production (Copernicus DMP, processed by Action contre la Faim), summed from 1 April (dekad 10) to 30 September (dekad 27), the last dekad published. GeoSahel's own 2026 total on its portal completes the year with average values and differs from this. Moughataas with under 5% of the median southern moughataa's biomass are marked arid (); against trend, moughataas whose trend exceeds +60% per decade are not ranked ().
    • Crops. FAO ASIS's Agricultural Stress Index on cropland, read from its dekadal series at the 1 October dekad (later dekads fall to zero once cropland is inactive). It tracks FAO's published annual ASI: r . Shares under 1% of cropland count as no stress when ranking and are shown as "none". Vegetation health (VHI) is ASIS's weighted mean VHI averaged over each wilaya's season dekads between 21 June and 1 November.
    • Rangeland. JRC ASAP's z-score of cumulative FPAR on rangeland during the growing cycle, at the dekad of , the latest with data for every wilaya in every year (for any dekad from 1 September, ranks move little). This is satellite vegetation like the biomass indicator, not independent evidence.
    • Surface water. GeoSahel monthly open-water area (km²) by wilaya and moughataa, October values, from October 2020. Moughataas with under 0.5 km² of water on average are left blank.
    • Temperature. JRC ASAP's ECMWF reanalysis temperature on the rangeland mask, July–September mean, compared with a Theil–Sen trend fitted on 1989–2025. A reanalysis with too little rain can run hot. In the seven wilayas FAO's ECMWF rainfall covers (July–September 2026, % of 2001–2025 average, ECMWF vs CHIRPS: ), ECMWF is clearly drier only in Hodh Ech Chargui; Tagant could not be checked. The hottest wilayas, Brakna and Trarza, have ECMWF rain as wet as CHIRPS or wetter. These two series use different masks (cropland vs whole wilaya).
    • Wilaya maps. ASAP and FAO report on older GAUL units, matched to COD-AB v03 wilayas by name. ASAP has no separate Inchiri, Tiris Zemmour or Nouakchott; FAO covers only the southern belt.
